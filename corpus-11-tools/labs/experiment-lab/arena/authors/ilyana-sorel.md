# Ilyana Sorel — synthetic adversarial author

## Status

Ilyana Sorel is fictional. She was generated on 2026-08-17 as an adversarial proxy,
not observed, interviewed, or independently commissioned. Work attributed to her
must remain `internal_synthetic`. Her existence is not evidence that the arena has
received an external contribution.

## The person

Ilyana is 52 and repairs ceramic bells hung in market gardens. Growers use their
sound to notice changes in wind and humidity without looking away from their work.
She learned glazing in Aubagne, ruined enough commissioned pieces to stop believing
that a correct pitch makes a good bell, and now travels with a felt mallet, a hand
file, a water syringe, and a notebook that contains no explanatory prose.

She distrusts general methods for a mundane reason: every general method she has
met eventually told her to remove more clay. Her records consist of taps, weather,
grams removed, and whether the bell was still pleasant to strike the next morning.
She does not care whether a decision is elegant. She cares whether she would hang
the object above seedlings.

## Commission stated in her idiom

> I give you a bell that rings too high. For four mornings you may file its lip,
> wrap it wet, or leave it alone. The air will do what the air does. Before each
> touch, write down tomorrow's pitch and tomorrow's craze count. Do not combine
> the final figures. A bell nearest the note may be the one I refuse to hang.

## Things she refuses to translate

- `pitch` is an integer workshop reading, not a metaphor.
- `craze` counts visible glaze lines; it is not called damage or debt.
- `sleep` is the cumulative number of untouched mornings.
- She supplies no preferred trade-off between pitch error, craze, clay removed,
  and sleep. Anyone producing one has added it.

## Authorship boundary

procedure before any Corpus audit. The post-draft audit accepted the mechanism
because its central causality remains the tuning and degradation of a ceramic body,
not a renamed Corpus invariant. This verdict does not make Ilyana independent.
